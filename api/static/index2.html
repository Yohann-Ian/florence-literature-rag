The name

Florence is named for the city where the Renaissance ignited, a beacon of the civilisation's enlightenment, where the recovery of ancient texts and the invention of new ones changed how humans read and reasoned.

What this does

Florence is a Retrieval-Augmented Generation system. Given a question, she:

  1. Checks the question is within her domain.
  2. Searches the indexed corpus for relevant passages.
  3. Grades each passage for relevance.
  4. Rewrites and retries if the search was weak.
  5. Generates an answer from the surviving passages.
  6. Self-checks the answer against the sources.
  7. Escalates to a human if confidence is low.

How is this different from Claude or ChatGPT?

I built this one myself. Chunking and embedding happen locally. The only times external LLMs are used are when I call on their APIs for interpretation of the chunks, presentation of language, and evaluation of faithfulness. The chunk retrieval, the agent orchestration, the retry loops, the human-in-the-loop mechanism, and the confidence grading are all handled with my code.

Florence was built from scratch, locally, then containerised and ported to AWS. The chat interface is the smallest visible part of a much larger machine.
